## Comparison over 13 years (to age 65)
- **A, all in ETF:** €20,000 + €900/month into a global ETF gives **≈ €217,000**
- **B, split:** €375/month (= €4,500/yr) into S-Pension (75–100% equity sub-fund, assumed fees 1–1.5%,
  payout taxed ~18%) + €525/month and the yearly tax refund (~€1,880) into the ETF gives **≈ €229,000–231,000**
- **Advantage of B: ≈ +€12,000 to +€14,000** (≈ +€19,000 if the horizon is 18 years).
  It stays positive unless S-Pension fees are well above 2%/yr.

## Retirement income at 65 (13 years), plan A
- Perpetual (3.5%/yr): ≈ €630/month · Spend down over 25 years: ≈ €920/month
- €2,000/month would need ≈ €470,000 (spend-down) to €686,000 (perpetual)
- **Gap is large.** The most important unknown is the expected **CNAP state pension**.

## Recommendation
1. Open **S-Pension**: €375/month into the **75% or 100% equity sub-fund**, after checking the sub-fund TER in the KID
   (accept ≤ ~1.5%). Switch to a lower-equity sub-fund around age 60–62.
2. Claim the deduction each year (tax return or *décompte annuel*). Expect a refund of ≈ €1,880/yr and invest it in the ETF.
3. €20,000 lump sum + €525/month into a global accumulating ETF (VWCE or WEBN).
4. Request a **CNAP pension estimate** (via MyGuichet) to size the real gap.
